The problem

Tumour type must be identified before treatment can be planned, but reading MRI is slow, needs scarce expertise, and varies between observers — most of all between glioma and meningioma, which can look alike on a single axial slice.

We classify one slice into four classes:

Glioma
Meningioma
No tumour
Pituitary

5,000 training images is far too few to train an 11-million-parameter network from scratch, so the whole method turns on how pretrained weights are adapted.

Greyscale → 3 channels Replicating the single channel lets the pretrained RGB filters apply unchanged.
3
Horizontal flip only Anatomy is near-symmetric, so a flip is plausible. Rotation and jitter were excluded — fabricating non-physical scans teaches artefacts, not pathology.
4
ImageNet normalisation Places inputs in the distribution the pretrained weights expect.

Method — why two phases

The new 4-class head starts random. Unfreezing everything at once would push large, noisy gradients back through the backbone and destroy the pretrained features before they could be used. Only layer4 is ever unfrozen: early layers already hold generic edge and texture detectors, while the deepest block holds the class semantics that must move from natural images to MRI.

Training — validation accuracy across both phases

Phase 1 plateaus at 84.2%: a frozen ImageNet backbone can only reach so far into a domain as distant as MRI. Unfreezing layer4 lifts validation accuracy to 95.3% in a single epoch, and past 97% by epoch 9. By epoch 13 it has converged: train sits at 99.9%+ while validation holds a narrow 97.7–98.1% band.

Results — per-class F1 on the held-out test set

Glioma and meningioma trail together — the first sign they are being confused with each other rather than failing independently. Macro and weighted F1 agree to within 0.002, so the headline is not an artefact of class imbalance.

What the errors say

63%of errors

Glioma ↔ meningioma is the one real weakness. 19 of 30 errors sit on this pair — also the hardest call for human readers on a single slice. The model fails where the task is genuinely ambiguous, not at random.

0missed

Perfect recall on no tumour. No scan containing a tumour was called tumour-free. This class's four errors all run the safe way, so false negatives — the costliest error in screening — did not occur.

99.3%recall

Pituitary is nearly separable. Two errors in 300. These tumours sit at a distinctive skull-base location, giving the network a positional cue the other tumour types do not offer.

Limits & next steps

Limits. One slice, not the volume. No localisation. A single dataset, so cross-scanner generalisation is unmeasured. Confidence is raw softmax and uncalibrated.

Next. Target the glioma/meningioma boundary with a focal loss or a second-stage binary head; add Grad-CAM; validate externally; aggregate across the slices of a study.
